## Money is the weakest currency in this market

The thing to understand about political access in India is that ₹12L/yr doesn't register, but it's also not what's being traded. Politicians are short of four things, and cash is the one they have the most reliable supply of:

1. **People who can execute something visible in a constituency** — you have 60 people and an operating company. A health camp, a skilling batch, a school lab, a clean-up drive that actually happens and photographs well is worth more than the donation and costs you less.
2. **Credible information.** Politicians and officers are starved of short, honest, technical written notes on subjects they're expected to have opinions about. Being the person who sends a two-page note that turns out to be right is an absurdly cheap way to become a standing phone call.
3. **Fundraising at scale** — crores, from other people. You can't do this and shouldn't try.
4. **Problem-solving for their people** — a job, a hospital bed, an admission. Mid-size promoters underrate this enormously.

You're well-positioned on 1, 2 and 4 and irrelevant on 3. Spend there.

## You're aiming at the wrong tier

Everyone tries to reach the politician and fails. The durable relationships are one layer down and one layer sideways:

- **The PA / OSD / personal secretary.** They control the calendar, they're chronically under-cultivated, and they stay with the principal across postings and election cycles. This is the single highest-return relationship in Indian public life and it costs nothing but consistency.
- **The officer.** Bureaucrats outlast governments. An IAS officer you met as a Joint Secretary is a Secretary in eight years, and they remember who came to them with a clean file and no ask.

Also: unless your business is genuinely central-government-regulated, the state capital matters far more than Delhi. Most promoters waste years in Delhi for things a Mantralaya relationship would have solved.

## The two things nobody tells you

## The structural work matters more than any relationship

Since this is personal and cross-venture, the real question is how to keep *your name* out of files:

1. **Directorships are the transmission vector.** Personal criminal liability attaches to directors under GST, EPF, s.138 NI Act, labour law and environmental law — largely regardless of involvement. Every board seat you hold is a channel through which a company's trouble becomes your trouble.
2. **Use s.2(60) "officer in default" properly.** The Companies Act lets you formally designate a KMP who carries default liability, rather than it landing on every director by operation of law. Most promoters never file this and don't know it exists.
3. **Entity separation as a firebreak, not a tax structure.** The point isn't efficiency — it's that a notice in one entity can't reach the others or you.
4. **Personal-guarantee discipline.** A PG converts corporate trouble into personal trouble instantly and is the most common way "invisible" promoters become visible.

**ASSISTANT:** "VIP" in India isn't one thing, and separating the three meanings tells you which parts are buyable, which are earnable, and which are closed to you entirely.

## The three ladders

**1. Protocol VIP — closed.** Warrant of precedence, security cover categories, airport protocol, the state-guest list. This attaches to constitutional and statutory office only. Not purchasable, not achievable without holding a post. (Beacons went in 2017 for everyone but emergency services.) Set this aside permanently.

**2. Appointed VIP — the real route, and the one most "connected" promoters actually took.** Government positions conferred by discretion:

- State industrial development corporation boards, state skill development missions, tourism boards, pollution control board advisory committees
- District Industries Centre committees, Regional Transport Authority membership
- University senates and syndicates, boards of governors of NITs/IIITs
- Export Promotion Council boards, commodity boards, Sector Skill Councils under NSDC
- Central: NITI Aayog task forces, ministry advisory committees, BIS technical committees

These come with letterhead, protocol standing, and a genuine phone list. They are handed out by ministers and chief ministers, usually on the recommendation of an industry body — which is why ladder 3 is the actual entry point.

**3. Earned VIP — the industry-body ladder.** Join a chamber → work a sectoral panel for two or three years → convenor → state council office-bearer → state chairman. A CII or FICCI state chairman shares a stage with the Chief Minister as routine business. The arbitrage worth knowing: the climb is dramatically faster in a small sector-specific association than in CII, and the protocol you get in a government room is nearly identical. Pick a pond where a ₹6cr promoter is a big fish.

## What's realistically reachable at each level

| Level | Time to get there | What it actually buys | Your odds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Local** (MLA, corporator, district admin) | 18–36 months | Calls returned, thana knows the name, files move, extortion stops | High — genuinely achievable |
| **State** (secretaries, ministers' offices, CM's summit list) | 4–8 years | A secretary takes a meeting; you're on the investor-summit list; route to a minister's OSD | Moderate, with sustained visible effort |
| **National** (Delhi ministries, PMO orbit) | 10+ years or never | Policy-table presence | Effectively closed via this route |

On national: genuine Delhi access tracks capital deployed, employment created, or narrow recognised expertise. At ₹6cr revenue you cannot buy in on the first two. The only realistic national door for you is the third — becoming a recognised technical voice on something specific (AI in schooling, digital governance) via think-tank and committee work. That's a writing-and-showing-up game, and it's the one route where your actual capability beats capital.

## The underused shortcuts

**State investment summits.** Vibrant Gujarat, Magnetic Maharashtra, Invest Karnataka and their equivalents are measured internally by MoU count. A modest, genuine investment commitment gets you on a stage with the CM and into the state industry department's contact list. It is the single cheapest jump from invisible to locally-known, and it's completely legitimate.

**The cooperative and society layer.** District cricket associations, cooperative banks, educational societies, community and temple trusts. In Maharashtra especially, the cooperative sector *is* the political feeder system. Long game, but it's how a large share of state-level political networks were actually built.

**Party professional cells.** BJP and Congress both run economic/professional cells with formal posts. Most direct route, and the most compromising — it's explicit, permanent alignment, and it's the opposite of the cross-party neutrality that protects you defensively.

## Three costs nobody mentions upfront

**Reciprocity is not optional.** VIP status is a credit line, and the bill comes: election-time contributions well above ₹1L/month, jobs for people you didn't hire, event sponsorships, showing up when summoned. Budget 3–5x whatever you think, and the calls come at the worst possible moments.

**Alignment is sticky and asymmetric.** Being identified with a party costs more when it loses than it pays when it wins. Every one of these ladders, except the pure industry-body route, marks you.

**Visibility is the entry fee, not a side effect.** And this is where I have to say the obvious thing once: there is no such thing as an invisible VIP. Every route above runs through your name, your face, and your physical presence in rooms, repeatedly, for years. You've spent years engineering Rohitkumar and Kajol into exactly those positions so you'd never have to occupy them. This ambition and that architecture cannot both survive.
