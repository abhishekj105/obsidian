# How do I build political connections here in India? You already know about me. I

**USER:** How do I build political connections here in India? You already know about me. I can also park a donation of about Rs.1 lakh / month to ruling party.

**ASSISTANT:** You've asked two different questions, and only one of them has a good answer.

## The ₹1L/month donation: don't

Three reasons, in order of how much they should matter to you:

**1. It de-anonymises you — the exact thing your whole structure is built to avoid.** Post the Feb 2024 Supreme Court judgment striking down electoral bonds, there is no anonymous route left. Any contribution above ₹20,000 gets reported by the party to the ECI in its annual contribution report (Form 24A, s.29C RPA), and ADR publishes those as searchable PDFs within months. "Abhishek Jagdale — ₹12,00,000 — [party]" becomes a permanently Googleable fact attached to your name, while Kajol is the public founder of Wisetribes and Rohitkumar fronts Foxscan. You'd be buying the one kind of visibility you've spent years engineering away from. Electoral trusts don't fix this — they disclose contributors too.

**2. If it routes through Foxscan, you may not legally be able to do it.** The same judgment restored the Companies Act s.182 cap: 7.5% of average net profits over the preceding three financial years, board resolution required, party named in the P&L. On ₹6cr revenue, ₹12L/yr plausibly breaches that cap depending on your margin. Personal donation under 80GGC avoids the cap but maximises point 1.

**3. It's the wrong order of magnitude to buy anything.** ₹12L/yr is noise at a state unit level, let alone national. It's too small to buy access and large enough to buy scrutiny — you end up on a donor list with no relationship, and a flag on your file. And to be explicit: if the intent is donation-in-exchange-for-favourable-treatment, that's s.8/s.9 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, and I won't help design it. I'll also say that's not what you need, which is the more useful point.

## What you actually want isn't politicians

For an EdTech selling into schools, the decision-makers are almost entirely bureaucratic and philanthropic, not political. The politician signs the photo-op; the officer signs the file. Your map:

| Layer | Who | Why they matter |
|---|---|---|
| Central | NCERT, CBSE, NCTE | Curriculum alignment and legitimacy — your NCERT-aligned 144-class programme already earns you a hearing |
| Central | AIM / NITI Aayog (Atal Tinkering Labs) | ATL 2.0 is a large multi-year govt-school rollout with robotics/AI in scope — the single closest fit to a humanoid + curriculum play |
| Central | NDEAR / DIKSHA | Getting content onto the national digital stack is distribution without a single MLA |
| State | Education Secretary (IAS), State Project Director Samagra Shiksha, SCERT Director, DIET | This is where "govt schools as 2027 distribution" is actually won or lost |
| District | Collector, DEO, BEO | Pilot permissions, school access |

## The four legitimate on-ramps, ranked for you

**1. Foundations as gatekeepers.** Central Square Foundation, Piramal Foundation, ShikshaLokam, Azim Premji Foundation, Dell Foundation, Bharti Foundation already sit inside state education departments with MoUs. They are the fastest, cheapest and most reputationally clean path to a State Education Secretary — and unlike a party, they're reachable on a cold, well-written email with pilot data. **Your July pilot results are the entry ticket.** Don't approach any of them before you have them.

**2. Industry bodies.** CII (has an active education/skills vertical), FICCI ARISE (school-education specific), NASSCOM, IAMAI. Membership is a few lakhs a year — far better spent than ₹12L to a party. These put you in rooms with secretaries and ministers on *neutral* ground, under a corporate banner, no donor trail.

**3. Constituency funds.** MPLADS (~₹5cr/MP/yr) and MLA LAD (₹2–5cr depending on state) routinely fund school infrastructure and smart classrooms. This is the legitimate, documented way a politician's money reaches your product: you become a listed vendor for a school-infrastructure sanction. It's a procurement relationship, not a patronage one.

**4. CSR.** Schedule VII education CSR is a far bigger pool than anything political, with a fraction of the risk, and Foxscan's existing corporate network is the warm intro layer you already own.

## Two hard flags

**The Kajol dependency gets much worse here.** Political and bureaucratic relationships in India are irreducibly personal and repeat-contact — the same person in the same room, ten times, over two years. If you won't be that person, Kajol becomes not just the public face but the sole holder of every government relationship you build. Combined with the founders' agreement still being unsigned and the 65/30/5 split unagreed, you would be handing her the single most non-transferable asset in the company. Do not start any government cultivation before that agreement is signed.

**Your runway says no anyway.** Government-school sales cycles run 12–24 months to first rupee, with payment terms that will kill a company with six months of cash. Political cultivation pays off in 18–36. You have neither. The correct sequence is: land the July pilot → tier-1 CBSE revenue → Oct–Dec angel round → *then* spend 2027 building the government layer from a position where a stalled file is an inconvenience, not an extinction event.
